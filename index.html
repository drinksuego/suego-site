the drink

three ingredients that shouldn’t taste this good.

suero, from the Latin for serum — a restorative drink that’s been around a long time and will be around longer. the kind of thing a grandmother in Mexico might make for a sick kid, or a host might pour at the start of a long dinner.

the salt cuts the lime’s bitterness. the lime cuts the salt’s edge. it tastes clean, a little surprising, a little familiar. people drink it instead of a cocktail, instead of a sparkling water, instead of a coconut water.

questions

the things people ask.

what does it taste like?

clean, a little salty, a little tart, not very sweet, lightly sparkling. closer to a topo chico with a squeeze of lime than to a la croix. tastes like itself after about two sips.

is it sweet?

barely. less than a gram of sugar per bottle. the sweetness comes from a tiny bit of organic cane and the lime — not from any kind of artificial sweetener, stevia, or monk fruit.

is it actually salty?

yes, lightly. about a quarter of the sodium of a typical sports drink. enough that you can taste it. it’s the salt that makes it interesting.

does it have caffeine or alcohol?

neither.

how is it different from other sparkling drinks?

la croix and topo chico are flavored sparkling waters. olipop is a functional soda. liquid death is canned water with personality. suego is none of those — it’s a sparkling suero, with real lime and real salt, rooted in a centuries-old drink. it tastes like itself.

where do you ship?

anywhere in the continental U.S. free over $40. ships in 2–4 days from california.
